## Description

*Apple asks for purpose, priority, specifics and objectives. Roughly 200 words is the
right length — editorial reads a lot of these.*

```
Cinch is a wardrobe app built on one observation: nobody finishes cataloguing their
closet. Every app in this category asks you to photograph a hundred garments before it
does anything for you, and most people quit somewhere around the twelfth.

Cinch removes the step. You walk your wardrobe and say what you own — "navy wool
crewneck," "black ankle boots." The app transcribes it, works out what each piece is,
and hands them back one at a time to confirm. A full closet takes about four minutes of
talking instead of half an hour of photography. Garments arrive as drawn silhouettes in
the app's own hand, never as catalogue photography, so nothing in your closet is a
picture of something you don't own.

What it does with that closet runs entirely on the phone. Outfit generation is a
deterministic engine — perceptual colour maths in OKLCH, a beam search over your
wardrobe, and a model that learns from every reaction. It works with no signal at all,
and it costs nothing per outfit. A language model is used for two things only: reading
a garment description, and writing one sentence about a look the engine already chose.

Our objective for launch is to reach people who have tried a wardrobe app before and
abandoned it during setup.
```

## Helpful Details

*This field is where accessibility, inclusivity and priority go. Apple's editorial team
demonstrably weights it — and in this case the accessibility story is real, not
retrofitted.*

```
ACCESSIBILITY

Voice intake is a genuine accessibility feature, not a convenience. Photographing two
hundred garments requires sustained fine motor control, good lighting, and the physical
ability to handle and stage each item. Describing them out loud requires none of that.
For users with limited mobility, chronic fatigue, low vision, or anything that makes a
half-hour photography session prohibitive, voice is the difference between a usable app
and an unusable one. Every garment is fully functional with no photograph ever attached.

The app is light-theme only by design, with all body text meeting WCAG AA contrast; a
mid-tone in the palette was overridden during design specifically because it failed at
3.5:1. Progress is always stated as a count — "four of twenty-three" — never a bar,
which keeps it legible to screen readers and to anyone who finds proportional indicators
hard to parse.

ON-DEVICE AND PRIVATE

The recommendation engine never leaves the phone. The model is never sent your wardrobe
— Edge Functions receive only a shortlist the device has already generated. Voice
recordings are transcribed and then deleted; Cinch keeps the words, never the audio.
There is no advertising and no tracking across other apps.

SEASONAL FIT

Launch coincides with back-to-school and the autumn wardrobe transition — the two
moments in the year when people most often look at a full closet and conclude they have
nothing to wear.

CRAFT

The interface is set in Instrument Serif and Archivo on a warm paper ground, with a
single accent. Garments the user has described rather than photographed are drawn as
silhouettes filled with their real colour, so a closet assembled by voice looks
deliberate rather than incomplete.
```

## After submitting

- The nomination saves as a **draft**. Go back in and hit **Submit** — this is the most
  common way a nomination silently never happens.
- ⚠️ CSV bulk uploads auto-submit and cannot be drafted. Use the individual form.
- Apple publishes no review timeline and no acceptance rate. You will only hear if it
  qualifies. Do not chase it.
- **File a second nomination later under App Enhancements** when the occasion work lands
  in November. Two nominations across a launch cycle is normal and each is judged on its
  own.
