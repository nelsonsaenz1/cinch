Privacy Policy

Cinch is a wardrobe app. It works by looking at photographs of your clothes, so this policy is mostly about photographs of your clothes. It describes what actually happens in the app, not what a template says usually happens.

The short version

  • Photos of the garments you add are uploaded and stored in your private account.
  • They are sent to an AI provider, OpenRouter, to identify what each item is. You are asked to agree to this before it happens the first time.
  • If you ask Cinch to find a shop's own photograph of a piece, a web search runs. It is carried out by Exa, not by OpenRouter, and the routing restriction Cinch sets on its AI requests does not cover it.
  • Cinch never reads your photo library. iOS shows you a picker and hands the app only the pictures you tap.
  • There is no advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, and nothing is sold or shared with data brokers.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app. It happens immediately.

What Cinch collects

Account information

When you sign in with Apple, Cinch receives your name and an email address. Apple may give you a private relay address instead of your real one, and Cinch works exactly the same either way. Your name is provided by Apple only on the very first sign-in and is stored at that moment or not at all.

You can also sign in with an email address instead. In that case Cinch receives the address you type and sends a sign-in code to it, and that address is what identifies your account.

Photographs of your clothes

Each garment you add is stored as an image in a private storage bucket that only your account can read. Images are never public unless you explicitly make a specific item public through a future sharing feature.

Details derived from those photographs

Category, colours, pattern, material, brand, how formal a piece is, how warm it is, and a one-line description. You can correct any of it.

Searching the web for a garment's listing — only if you ask

Cinch can put a shop's own photograph of a piece on your garment, so a coat you photographed on a hanger looks like the coat. You can also add a piece by describing it, or by pasting the link it came from, instead of photographing it. Nothing here happens on its own: a search runs only when you start one, and a picture is saved only when you pick it.

Two different things leave the app for this, and they are worth keeping apart.

Matching a piece you photographed. Cinch creates a link to your own photograph that works for five minutes, and sends it, with a line of the garment's details, to an AI model that has a web search attached, so the model can look for where the piece is sold.

Describing a piece instead. The words you type — "black midi dress, Reformation" — are sent as a web search query. No photograph goes with them unless you attach one to show the true colour, and that one is shrunk to 256 pixels on your phone before it is sent and is never stored by Cinch.

The search is not run by the AI provider. It is run by Exa, a search company, on OpenRouter's behalf. This matters, and we would rather write it down than let it be assumed: Cinch tells OpenRouter to route its AI requests only to model providers that are not allowed to train on what they receive, and that restriction does not extend to the search. OpenRouter says so itself — its data-retention enforcement "does not apply to plugins and tools you choose to enable, such as web search". So the words a search is made of, and the pages that come back, are handled under Exa's own policy, not under anything Cinch can hold anyone to on your behalf.

Cinch's server then reads the shop pages that came back and downloads the picture you choose. While you are looking at the results, though, your phone loads those pictures from the shops' own servers, exactly as a web page would — so those shops see a request from your device. They are told nothing about you or your closet, and Cinch has no arrangement with any of them: no affiliate links, no commission, and no way to buy anything in the app.

When you match a piece you had already photographed, Cinch records which shop the picture came from and prints it on the garment. Removing the listing photograph brings back your own, which is never altered or deleted.

A recording of your voice — only if you describe your closet out loud

Cinch lets you build your closet by talking instead of photographing. If you choose to, the app records you describing your clothes and uploads that recording to a private storage bucket only your account can read.

The recording is sent to a third-party model provider to be turned into text. It is deleted as soon as that succeeds — Cinch keeps the words, never the audio. If the transcription fails, the recording is kept so you can retry without describing everything again, and it is deleted automatically within 24 hours whether or not you come back. Deleting your account removes any recording immediately.

The text is then read by a model to work out which garments you named. Unlike outfit suggestions — where a model only ever sees a shortlist the app already built on your phone — this is a path where a model does receive a description of your wardrobe, because that is the whole of what you asked it to read. The resulting transcript is kept as part of your closet, so each garment can show you the words it came from.

Approximate location — only if you allow it

Used to fetch the local forecast so outfit suggestions suit the weather. Coordinates are rounded to roughly 1.1 kilometres before they leave your device and are not stored. If you decline, Cinch falls back to a default city and tells you on screen that it has done so.

Calendar event titles — only if you allow it

If you connect your calendar, Cinch reads the titles and times of today's events so it can suggest one outfit that works across all of them. All-day entries are ignored. Event titles are included in the request to the AI provider. Cinch does not read past or future days, does not read event notes, attendees or locations, and does not store your calendar.

What you type

Anything you write about your plans, such as "dinner at 7 then drinks", is sent to the AI provider along with the outfit options so it can pick sensibly.

How you use the app

Which outfits you like, dislike, and wear; when you wore them; and the reasons you give. This is what the app learns your taste from. Cinch also keeps a technical record of each AI request — tokens used, cost, response time, and whether it succeeded — to monitor reliability and spend.

What Cinch does not collect

  • Your photo library. The app has no permission to browse it and never requests one.
  • Precise location. It is rounded before transmission and never stored.
  • Advertising identifiers, or any identifier used to track you across other companies' apps and websites.
  • Contacts, microphone, health data, or financial information.

This website, and the waitlist on it

These pages set no cookies and run no JavaScript at all, so there is no analytics here, no tracking pixel, and nothing to opt out of.

If you leave your email address in the form on the home page, that address is stored so we can write to you once about Cinch. It is not connected to any Cinch account, it is not sold or shared, and it is not added to any other list. Nothing is sent to you automatically, including a confirmation.

That form is public and unauthenticated, so it has to be protected from being flooded. To do that, the IP address of the request is combined with a secret and hashed, and only the hash is kept, next to a count of recent submissions from it. The IP address itself is never stored and never reaches the database. Those counters are deleted after seven days.

Ask at the address below and your entry on the waitlist is deleted.

Consent for AI processing

Before any photograph is sent to an AI provider, Cinch asks you in the app and explains what is sent. If the providers or the categories of data change, you will be asked again rather than being opted in silently.

That consent covers every door a photograph can leave by: tagging, matching a piece against a product listing, and the colour swatch you can attach to a description. If you withdraw it, all of them stop, and so does the stylist chat. Outfit suggestions carry on — those are built on your phone and never needed the AI.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it. There is no automatic expiry, because a wardrobe is meant to be a long-lived record. Delete your account and everything goes — the photographs in storage as well as the rows in the database.

One thing does expire on its own: a voice recording. It is deleted the moment the transcription succeeds, and within 24 hours regardless. That is the only piece of your data Cinch destroys without being asked, and it is destroyed because keeping it was never the point — the words were.

Deleting your account

Open Profile → Delete account. This removes every photograph you uploaded, every garment record, your outfit history, any voice recording that has not already been deleted, and everything the app has learned about your style. It happens immediately and cannot be undone. You do not need to email anyone.

Your rights

Depending on where you live — including the UK, the European Economic Area, and California — you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data, and to object to certain processing. Deletion is available directly in the app. For anything else, contact us at the address below and we will respond within 30 days.

Cinch does not sell personal information and does not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are defined under California law.

Children

Cinch is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will remove it.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Garment images live in a private bucket with per-user access rules enforced at the database level, so one account cannot read another's files. Image links given to the AI provider expire after five minutes. No system is perfectly secure, and we do not claim otherwise.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top will change and — where the change affects what is sent to AI providers — you will be asked to agree again inside the app.
