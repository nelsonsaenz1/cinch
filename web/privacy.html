Privacy Policy

Cinch is a wardrobe app. It works by looking at photographs of your clothes, so this policy is mostly about photographs of your clothes. It describes what actually happens in the app, not what a template says usually happens.

The short version

  • Photos of the garments you add are uploaded and stored in your private account.
  • They are sent to an AI provider, OpenRouter, to identify what each item is. You are asked to agree to this before it happens the first time.
  • Cinch never reads your photo library. iOS shows you a picker and hands the app only the pictures you tap.
  • There is no advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, and nothing is sold or shared with data brokers.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app. It happens immediately.

What Cinch collects

Account information

When you sign in with Apple, Cinch receives your name and an email address. Apple may give you a private relay address instead of your real one, and Cinch works exactly the same either way. Your name is provided by Apple only on the very first sign-in and is stored at that moment or not at all.

Photographs of your clothes

Each garment you add is stored as an image in a private storage bucket that only your account can read. Images are never public unless you explicitly make a specific item public through a future sharing feature.

Details derived from those photographs

Category, colours, pattern, material, brand, how formal a piece is, how warm it is, and a one-line description. You can correct any of it.

Approximate location — only if you allow it

Used to fetch the local forecast so outfit suggestions suit the weather. Coordinates are rounded to roughly 1.1 kilometres before they leave your device and are not stored. If you decline, Cinch falls back to a default city and tells you on screen that it has done so.

Calendar event titles — only if you allow it

If you connect your calendar, Cinch reads the titles and times of today's events so it can suggest one outfit that works across all of them. All-day entries are ignored. Event titles are included in the request to the AI provider. Cinch does not read past or future days, does not read event notes, attendees or locations, and does not store your calendar.

What you type

Anything you write about your plans, such as "dinner at 7 then drinks", is sent to the AI provider along with the outfit options so it can pick sensibly.

How you use the app

Which outfits you like, dislike, and wear; when you wore them; and the reasons you give. This is what the app learns your taste from. Cinch also keeps a technical record of each AI request — tokens used, cost, response time, and whether it succeeded — to monitor reliability and spend.

What Cinch does not collect

  • Your photo library. The app has no permission to browse it and never requests one.
  • Precise location. It is rounded before transmission and never stored.
  • Advertising identifiers, or any identifier used to track you across other companies' apps and websites.
  • Contacts, microphone, health data, or financial information.

Who else processes your data

Cinch uses a small number of providers. Each receives only what it needs to do its job.

ProviderWhat it receivesWhy
Supabase Your account, garment records, and images Database, file storage, and sign-in. Hosted in the United States.
OpenRouter Garment images via a link that expires after five minutes; the weather; your plans; event titles AI tagging and outfit reasoning. OpenRouter passes requests to the underlying model provider that serves the chosen model.
Open-Meteo Rounded coordinates only — no account, no identifier Weather forecast.
Apple Sign-in credentials Sign in with Apple. Governed by Apple's own privacy policy.

Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared for advertising. Cinch has no relationship with any data broker.

Consent for AI processing

Before any photograph is sent to an AI provider, Cinch asks you in the app and explains what is sent. If the providers or the categories of data change, you will be asked again rather than being opted in silently.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it. There is no automatic expiry, because a wardrobe is meant to be a long-lived record. Delete an item and its images are removed. Delete your account and everything goes.

Deleting your account

Open Profile → Delete account. This removes every photograph you uploaded, every garment record, your outfit history, and everything the app has learned about your style. It happens immediately and cannot be undone. You do not need to email anyone.

Your rights

Depending on where you live — including the UK, the European Economic Area, and California — you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data, and to object to certain processing. Deletion is available directly in the app. For anything else, contact us at the address below and we will respond within 30 days.

Cinch does not sell personal information and does not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are defined under California law.

Children

Cinch is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will remove it.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Garment images live in a private bucket with per-user access rules enforced at the database level, so one account cannot read another's files. Image links given to the AI provider expire after five minutes. No system is perfectly secure, and we do not claim otherwise.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top will change and — where the change affects what is sent to AI providers — you will be asked to agree again inside the app.
