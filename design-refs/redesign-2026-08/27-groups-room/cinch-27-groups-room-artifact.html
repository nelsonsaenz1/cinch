Groups — a private table, not a settings list
Fifty-two states of a group that nobody outside can find. The room leads with the people; invitations arrive as warm bands; the roster is withheld until acceptance and never attributes a removal. Frames are 393×852pt, exported at 790×1708.
Refusals held in this design A pending invitee sees a count, never the roster Removal is never attributed — to the removed person or anyone else Blocking is never named; a broken mutual reads as "no longer a mutual friend" No discovery, no avatar, no rename, no chat — nothing the model does not hold "Circle" appears nowhere in customer-facing text

Implementation handoff
Routes & components/groups (list), /groups/create, /groups/[circleId] (room), /groups/[circleId]/invite, /groups/[circleId]/manage — all existing, none added. Presentation only: the room becomes a table of authorized portraits rather than a settings list, invitations become warm bands, and roster grammar is carried by structure instead of paragraphs. Internal identifiers keep the circle_* naming; no customer-facing string uses the word "circle".What a group isName, owner, membership. Nothing else exists and nothing else is drawn: no avatar, cover, description, discoverable mode, feed, chat, moderator, co-owner, ownership transfer, search, recommendation, nearby or popular surface, invite code, or rename. Where a reader might expect one of these, the design shows nothing rather than a disabled affordance.Two to twelveA liveness promise, not a SQL floor. A newly created group legally holds only its owner and renders as a complete, correct state — never an error or an empty apology. The owner counts toward twelve; joined members and pending invitations both consume seats. The full-group fact appears only where an owner acts on it: the room header and the invite picker. An ordinary member never meets a capacity wall.Roster visibilityA pending invitee sees the group name, the inviter, and a scalar joined count — and no more. The withheld roster is drawn as neutral silhouettes with a sentence saying identity arrives on acceptance, so the refusal is legible rather than looking like a loading failure. Nobody outside the group can discover it exists; there is no route, search or surface that would allow it.Invitation eligibilityOnly the owner invites, and only from existing mutual friends. Ineligible candidates stay visible with a truthful reason: already a member, invited, no longer a mutual friend, or group full. Blocking is never named as a cause — a block makes the pair non-mutual, and "no longer a mutual friend" is the honest surface for every reason a mutual ended.Removal anonymityAbsolute. The removed person is told they are no longer in the group and never who removed them; there is no timestamp, actor, or activity trail that could identify one. The removal confirmation states this guarantee to the owner too, so the promise is visible from both sides. Remaining members see the roster shrink with no attribution.Membership statesinvited, joined, declined, removed, left. Declined and left are not terminal — both appear under a neutral "Not in the group" heading in Manage with an invite-again action, because the model allows re-invitation and the UI should not imply otherwise. The owner can remove or re-invite but cannot force anyone to join.DeletionDeleting a group deletes the group and its invitations. It deletes no person, garment, outfit, wear record or friendship, and the confirmation says so in its second sentence — the first sentence states the consequence, the second bounds it.Identity dataRoster entries come only from authorized social profile data. A missing authorized name or portrait degrades to a neutral Member treatment with an initial-free silhouette; no public profile is queried and no identity is inferred or invented to fill the gap.Failure & stalenessA read failure is never the empty state: it says the group is safe and offers retry in place. Offline shows what the device already held and labels it, so an absent person is not misread as a departure. A stale invite list produces a per-row outcome — one sent, one refused — rather than a single misleading success, and offers a refresh rather than a blind retry. Every destructive failure states that nothing changed.Analytics allowlistBounded enums and integers only: groups_view { surface: list | room | invite | manage }, group_action { action: create | invite | accept | decline | remove | reinvite | leave | delete, outcome: ok | failed, reason: full | not_mutual | stale | network }, group_counts { members, invited } as integers. Forbidden in every payload: group name, group identifier, member identifier, member name, avatar path or URL, inviter identity, roster contents, and free text of any kind.AccessibilityFocus order: back → group name → ownership and count → each person as one node → each invited person as one node → primary action → owner controls → notices. Invited status is spoken, never conveyed by tint alone. Targets are ≥44pt and ≥56pt at AX sizes; Dynamic Type grows portraits and names together and wraps rather than truncating, because a truncated eligibility reason is worse than none. Sheets trap focus; pending writes lock their action without trapping the screen. Removed prose is preserved in labels — the privacy meanings the layout implies are spoken in full.MotionDefault: the table settles in with a short stagger and rows fade on change. Reduce Motion presents the table complete with no travel and no stagger, and removes nothing informational.TestsNo customer-facing string contains "circle". A pending invitee cannot read the roster through any state, including offline and error. A one-member group renders as legal rather than empty. Owner counts toward twelve; pending invitations consume seats; the full fact appears only on owner surfaces. Only mutual friends appear as candidates; each ineligibility reason renders truthfully and none names blocking. Removal never attributes an actor in copy, labels or analytics. Declined and left members can be re-invited. Delete removes the group only. Read failure renders distinctly from empty on all four surfaces. Partial invite results report per row. Destructive failures state that nothing changed. No analytics payload carries a name, identifier or roster. AX-XXXL, 667pt, VoiceOver order and Reduce Motion all verified.DeviationsThree, all in favour of repo truth over the brief. (1) The brief’s "roster grammar preserved exactly" is honoured by structure — joined and invited are separate labelled regions with different verbs (remove vs withdraw) — rather than by carrying explanatory paragraphs into the room. (2) No rename control appears anywhere, because none ships today and drawing one would promise it. (3) The empty-group and withheld-roster states use faceless silhouettes rather than generic avatars, so no invented identity is ever rendered.ShippingPresentation-only over existing routes, RPCs and RLS: no new schema, grant, package, vendor, permission, native module, data recipient or public surface.
