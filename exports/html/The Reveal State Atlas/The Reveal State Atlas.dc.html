Cinch · Directive 2 · the reveal · build reference
The Reveal state atlas
The tenth piece lands and the app says what it can now do: ten pieces, fourteen outfits, followed by the first real outfit built from clothes photographed minutes ago. Both numbers come from the counting function, not from copy. This screen never mentions upgrading — asking for money on top of a gift is how a good moment turns cheap.
01 · The sequence
Three beats, one screen
The reveal is not a modal that appears and is dismissed. It is one surface that resolves in three beats and then becomes the first Today screen the user ever sees.
beat 1 · arrival
The tenth settles
The last garment finishes processing and joins the other nine. The count is not known yet — the engine is still building — and the screen does not pretend otherwise or show a spinner where a number will be.
beat 2 · the count
Ten pieces. Fourteen outfits.
Set in the serif at the largest size anywhere in the app. The number is whatever the engine will actually suggest — never padded with combinations it would refuse. If the honest count is nine, the screen says nine.
beat 3 · the proof
One of them, laid out
A claim followed immediately by evidence: a real outfit, from their garments, with the colophon the rest of the app uses. The proof is what makes the number credible.

03 · Rules for the build
What must stay true
No upsell on this screen
No price, no trial, no “imagine with thirty”. The gap feed and the wall exist and can wait.
The count is suggestible outfits
Combinations the engine would refuse are not counted. The number on the reveal and the number of plates the user can actually reach must never diverge.
Show the working
Every count is followed by the sentence that derives it. A number with no derivation is indistinguishable from a slogan.
It ends in Today
The reveal is not dismissed — it becomes the first Today screen, so the app the user lands in is the app they were just shown.
