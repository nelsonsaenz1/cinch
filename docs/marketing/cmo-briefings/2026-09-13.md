# LAYER 2 — the thesis

## 7. Tonight's thesis — *the machine finally ran on strangers, and the first three readings say the ask is not the variable either*

**What kind of business is Cinch tonight?** A $20-a-day paid funnel that this weekend turned five
installs a day into four accounts, two and a half of those into a ten-piece closet, one of those into a
completed Reveal, and one of those into an ask — and none of the asks into money. Every stage above the
last one is the healthiest it has ever read: the first-item rate is 68% on a 38-person week, ten-piece
closets tripled, four strangers completed a Reveal in 26 hours after nobody had in the 61 before. The
ask is where the funnel still ends, and for the first time in the product's life it ends there for a
reason that is not wiring, not price plumbing, not a trial-less button, and not a write failure. Three
people were shown five outfits from their own clothes and then, twelve to sixty seconds later, a $0.99
sheet with a seven-day free year on the button. Three closed it.

**Is the ask the binding constraint?** I am not allowed to say so on n=3 (METHODOLOGY: twenty people,
three days), and I will not. What I can say is which excuses died this weekend. "The ask precedes the
payoff" — dead on ship 10 (P30, 0 of 3). "Nobody with a dressable closet arrives" — dead (five ten-piece closets in 26 h).
"The price is the barrier" — the price fell 80% on 09-11 and the first three answers at the new price
are the same as the seventeen at the old one. "The default button has no trial" — closed 09-09. What is
left is the product's own claim: that a person who has just seen five outfits from ten photographs wants
more of that badly enough to type a passcode. Three data points say not yet. Twenty will say whether.

**The other thing the weekend moved, quietly, is the come-back bin.** It held (P17, P29 — 0 of 21 at
≥48 h), and I keep scoring it, but for three weeks the bin held because *nobody* came back, and this
weekend three people did: one at +45.6 h who finished his ten and reacted to five outfits, one at +103 h
for seven seconds, one at +127 h for thirteen seconds — the last of whom was shown a paywall two seconds
after the app opened, over five garments that had never been dressed. That is the shape of the second
day tonight: not that people do not return, but that when they do, **Today has nothing for a five-garment
closet except an ask, and nothing for a ten-garment closet except what it showed yesterday.** The returns
are small (3 of 44), and two of the three lasted less time than the paywall's animation. The product does
not yet have a second day; it has a first day that some people re-open.

**Evidence against.** (a) n=3 on the ask and n=3 on the returns; the 4-of-4 → 6-of-6 come-back record was
built on cohorts of 8–13 and has now seen its first ≥45 h returners. (b) The three post-Reveal impressions

the day-3 `returning_free` was designed for exactly that person; it fired this weekend on the wrong one.

built, and stayed. (d) `7206d770` — the embedded-bundle person asked before her Reveal — still finished
the Reveal after the sheet; the order may matter less than three theses claimed.

impressions — then the ask converts at a rate this volume simply cannot show, and the job is volume. Or
