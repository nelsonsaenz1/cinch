## Layer 2 — the thesis

### 7. Tonight's thesis — the constraint moved to retention, and I can prove the previous two theses were both incomplete

For five nights this document has argued about which half of the top of the funnel was broken. On
09-03 I said arrivals. On 09-05 I retracted that and said the listing — impressions were healthy
and the tap was not. Tonight arrivals went up roughly 6× **without anyone shipping a listing
change** (screenshot set v3, night 10; icon unchanged; promotional text unchanged), and the
business did not move at all. That is the most useful fact this document has produced in a week,
because it is a natural experiment neither thesis predicted.

Here is what 15 arrivals in 48 hours bought. Eighteen people picked a photo. Three built a full
ten-piece closet inside a single session — Parkville 10, Fennville 11, Champaign 14. Six people
saw a paywall, across four distinct placements, including the first-ever `ten_completed` and
`reveal_complete` presentations in the product's history. Two people turned on notifications.
Twelve real closets now hold ten or more garments. Nothing failed: no import errors, no cutout
failures, no support mail, no complaints.

And **D1 retention on the 09-05 cohort of eight is zero.**

So the thesis changes a third time in six nights, and I want to be honest that a thesis changing
three times is usually a sign the author is chasing the last data point. This one is different in
a specific way I can name: the previous two theses were both about *getting people in*, and both
were arguments from a denominator so small that nothing downstream was falsifiable. Tonight the
denominator quadrupled and the downstream numbers finally became readable. What they say is that
**Cinch converts arrivals into closets well and closets into returns not at all.** The
install→first-item rate is 41.9%, which is respectable. The first-item→second-day rate is
approximately zero, and that is the number the business dies on.

**What kind of business is Cinch tonight?** It is a product that people will genuinely do fifteen
minutes of work for — photographing ten garments is not a casual act, and three strangers did it
unprompted in two days — and that gives them no reason to open it again tomorrow morning. The two
mechanisms that would give them one are push (2 tokens, 0 sends ever) and the Morning Edit, and
push is exactly the thing 11 of 13 people declined to engage with. The binding constraint is
**day-2 return**, and it is the first constraint in six weeks that is squarely a product problem
rather than a measurement or a store problem.

**Evidence against this thesis, stated plainly.** Three things could unmake it. First, the surge
may be an artefact of something I cannot see — if ASC shows these as Browse impressions from a
category placement, they are lower-intent than search arrivals and their retention says less than
I am claiming. Second, D1 windows for the 09-06 cohort are incomplete and one person has already
returned. Third, and most seriously: **eight is a small cohort, and a D1 of 0/8 is consistent
with a true D1 rate as high as ~20%.** I am reading a strong claim off a thin denominator, which
is the exact error this document has retracted three times. Treat "retention is the constraint"
as the best available reading, not as established.

**Is $50K MRR by December still live? No.** Nothing tonight changes that; if anything the surge
sharpens it, because we now know what a good arrival day produces in revenue, and the answer is
zero. **The 1 November verdict is still live**, and tonight reorders what the next eight weeks
should test. Condition 1 (install→paid) is finally *testable* — 12 closets at ten-plus garments is
a real paywall audience for the first time. Conditions 2 and 3 are untouched.

**What changed my thesis tonight:** arrivals quadrupled and every downstream number improved
