# Layer 2 — the thesis

## Tonight's thesis: Cinch has a world-class engineering rail bolted to an ops rail that has never moved

Fifteen hours ago this briefing argued the binding constraint was signup→first-item, and
that the Reveal had been a three-night distraction. That thesis survives tonight and gets
something it lacked: a mechanism, and a name for the step.

The step is `closet_floor_shown` → `clothes_import_selected`. Thirty-one people were shown
the floor that asks for ten pieces; **thirteen** of them opened the picker and **eighteen
never did** (a fourteenth selector reached the picker without the floor event). Supabase
agrees independently — 31 real profiles in 14 days, 14 holding at least one garment. A13
asked for instrumentation to find this; the instrumentation was already there and nobody
had looked.

The mechanism is not, as I assumed last night, purely motivational. Tonight it is at least
partly **failure**. `ea34762e` did not lose interest — he tried thirty-one times over
fourteen hours, on the build Apple approved yesterday, and twenty of those attempts came
back `row_write_failed`. He got twelve garments in. Then the app told him he was over the
limit and asked him for money. If any meaningful share of the seventeen who stalled at the
floor met a version of that, the "56% never bothered" story I told last night was
uncharitable to our own users.

But the sharper number is the one beside it. `today_opened` = **35**. `stylist_opened` =
**28**. `clothes_import_selected` = **14**. More people open the Today tab and the Stylist
than ever open the photo picker. They are wandering an empty house. Cinch shows strangers
its destination — the morning answer, the stylist — before it has anything of theirs to
work with, and an empty version of a magic trick is indistinguishable from a broken app.
That is a design decision, not a bug, and it is the one I would change if I could change
one thing.

Underneath both sits the fact this run makes unavoidable. Since last night's directive,
**ten commits shipped, two OTAs published, a binary went to review — and zero humans were
contacted.** Savannah has waited 42 hours; the free year of Pro Nelson promised her on 8/30
is still ungranted while she sits on 108 items and reactions the product cannot read; two
5★ reviewers have waited four days; the promotional text — the one store lever that needs
no review and can be changed nightly — is unchanged for a fourth night; two Dispatch drafts
sit in Resend while 22 trials expire in ten days. This is not a resourcing problem. Code
is what this organization does when it is anxious. **The binding constraint on the
*business* is no longer a funnel step; it is that the only rail with throughput is the one
that cannot, by itself, produce a customer.**

**Is $50K MRR by December still live? No** — unchanged and not close. **Is the 1 Nov
verdict still live? Yes**, and tonight sharpens its reading. Today a stranger was asked for
money by Cinch for the first time in two days, through a placement nobody planned, seconds
after twenty failures. The November question is not "how many downloads" but "can this
company ask a stranger for money at a moment that makes sense to the stranger." Today it
could not.

**What would change my mind:** two things, either one. A production purchase reconciling —
that still tests the whole machine. Or the ops queue emptying in a day: replies sent, Pro
granted, Dispatch out, promo text swapped. If that happens once, the thesis above is wrong
and the real constraint is back inside the product.
