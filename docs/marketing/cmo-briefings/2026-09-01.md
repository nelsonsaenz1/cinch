# Layer 2 — the thesis

## Tonight's thesis: the constraint was never the Reveal, and tonight proves it twice over

For three nights this briefing has argued, in escalating language, that the Reveal was
the thing standing between Cinch and revenue. Tonight that argument dies from both ends.

From the top: the Reveal's entire production audience over the last ten days is **two
people**. Three humans have ever seen `reveal_door_shown`, and one of them is a dev
build. Even a perfect Reveal, converting at 100%, moves the business by two. Every
sentence written about the Reveal since 8/26 was an argument about a rounding error, and
the reason it felt urgent is that it was the *last* step visible in the funnel — the
place where the numbers finally got small enough to look broken. They were small when
they arrived.

From the bottom: tonight the shoeless-cores fix shipped and the Reveal ran
`arriving → settled → complete` end to end for the first time since 2026-08-21. That is a
real fix and it should be said plainly. It is also, per the evidence standard, **not
production evidence**: the person is `115d6391`, `$app_build = "1"`, 311 events across
every surface of the app in 90 minutes — the journey harness. **Zero production users
have completed a Reveal since 8/21.** The same caveat applies to tonight's five
`paywall_presented` events; they are the harness, not customers.

So what is the constraint? **34 people signed up in the last 14 days and 20 of them never
photographed one garment.** Not "never finished the Ten" — never added a single item.
That is a 56% loss at the very first act the product asks for, against a population ten
times larger than every downstream stage combined. Cinch's whole thesis is that
cataloging is the cliff other apps fall off; the people who climb it here are genuinely
served (14 past the Ten, 5.0★ reviews, a 108-item closet). We have just never looked hard
at the majority who put the phone down before item one.

Underneath that sits the deeper problem: **the app cannot learn.** 42 love/hate taps from
7 users have gone into a table nothing reads. Cinch is a suggestion engine with amnesia.
This is the single most dangerous fact in the briefing, because it is not a growth
problem — it is the reason a user who *does* climb the cliff eventually leaves. Acloset,
with 2,900 reviews, is being punished in public for exactly this ("suggesting a linen
spaghetti strap dress with a puffer when the weather shows 20°F"), while Cinch's own 5★
review praises the opposite ("it takes so many different things into account like
weather"). We are currently earning that praise by luck, not by mechanism.

**Is $50K MRR by December still live? No.** At $11 blended ARPU that is ~4,500 paying
subscribers. Cinch has 45 accounts, 38 first-time downloads in 30 days, and zero
purchases in its history. Nothing in tonight's data makes that arithmetic reachable, and
continuing to plan against it costs credibility that the 1 Nov gate needs. **The 1 Nov
verdict is still live and is now the right instrument** — but it should be re-read as a
question about *mechanism*, not scale: by 1 Nov, has a stranger paid Cinch money without
being asked to by Nelson? That question is answerable at this volume. $50K is not.

**What would change my mind:** one reconciled production purchase. It would prove every
link between wanting the product and Apple charging a card — the part that has never once
executed. That, not the download count, is what November should test.
