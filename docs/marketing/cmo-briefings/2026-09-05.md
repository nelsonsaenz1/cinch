## Layer 2 — the thesis

### 7. Tonight's thesis — I was wrong on Wednesday, and the falsifier I named is the one that fired

Two nights ago I wrote that Cinch had stopped being a monetization-blocked business and
become a distribution-starved one: "The binding constraint is now, plainly, **arrivals** —
three installs a day." I then wrote down what would change my mind:

> "I would change my mind if the ASC key landed and showed impressions holding near the
> post-rename level while downloads collapsed — that would make this a tap-through problem,
> not a demand problem, and it would resurrect A4."

Tonight ASC read, and that is exactly what it shows. Impressions on 09-04: **837**, the
strongest day since 08-29 and higher than every day of the preceding week. Impressions over
30 days: **7,139**. Product page views over the same window: **250**. First-time downloads:
**57**. Apple is putting Cinch in front of roughly 600–800 people a day and **3.5 of every
hundred tap through**. Of those who reach the page, **22.8% install** — a perfectly ordinary
rate. The demand is there and the page-to-install step works. The listing is losing it.

So the thesis changes again, and I want to name that it has now changed twice in five nights,
because that is a pattern worth distrusting. But this change is different in kind from the
last one: the last was a reading of silence, and this is a reading of a number I had never
seen. For five briefings I recorded impressions as "unread — no API key," and A21 (create an
ASC API key) sat at the top of the board as the thing blocking the business's most important
measurement. **A21 was never required.** The endpoint the sessions kept calling,
`/analytics/api/v1/data/time-series`, is a dead hyphenated path that 410s. The live one is
`/analytics/api/v1/data/timeseries`, and the dashboard renders the whole acquisition funnel
in a browser tab whenever the session is alive. Five nights of the most important question in
the business went unanswered because of a hyphen. That is the most expensive measurement
error in this document's history and it is mine.

**What kind of business is Cinch tonight?** It is a business with working demand, a working
paywall, a working import path, and a storefront that converts at roughly half the rate it
needs to. It is not distribution-starved in the sense of nobody looking. It is
**presentation-starved**: 7,139 people were shown Cinch this month and 6,889 of them did not
care enough to tap. The second constraint, immediately behind it, is activation — the three
most recent signups hold zero garments between them, and D1 retention has been at or near
zero since 08-29. Those are two different problems and the first one gates the second: there
is no point improving activation for four people a day.

**Evidence against this thesis, stated plainly.** I cannot yet see the *source* of those
impressions. If most of the 7,139 are Browse impressions — Cinch surfacing in unrelated
category lists rather than in searches for "outfit planner" — then a 3.5% tap-through is
normal and the listing is not underperforming at all. That single breakdown, two clicks deep
in ASC, decides whether A4 is the right call or a waste of a review cycle. It is the top item
in Data Gaps and it should be read before the screenshots are cut. What would also change my
mind: a new screenshot set landing and moving nothing.

**Is $50K MRR by December still live? No** — and nothing tonight softens that. Zero paying
users on 5 September, 4 downloads a day, and a paywall that has been shown to exactly two
people. There is no schedule of conversions that reaches $50K in fourteen weeks. **The 1
November verdict is still live**, and tonight sharpens where the next eight weeks should go:
condition 1 (install→paid) cannot be tested at four installs a day, so the work is conditions
2 and 3 — and A4 is the cheapest lever on either, because it multiplies every install the
store already wants to give us.

What changed my thesis tonight was not a new failure. It was finding out that I had been
guessing at the most important number in the business for five nights when it was one URL away.

#### 8.3 The auth-cancel claim — stated, tested, and withdrawn

**Claim considered:** the Apple sign-in sheet is failing and reporting a cancel, so
`auth_failed {reason:'canceled'}` is eating arrivals. Supporting shape: 11 persons / 16

**Falsification query** — if these are genuine dismissals, cancellers should mostly leave; if
