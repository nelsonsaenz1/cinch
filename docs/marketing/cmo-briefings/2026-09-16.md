## METHODOLOGY amendments made tonight

1. **ASC `units` publishes later than `impressionsTotal` and `pageViewCount` — do not read "no units row" as
   "zero downloads."** On 2026-09-16 at 23:1xZ the daily table carried 09-15 impressions (245) and page views
   (15) while `units` for 09-15 read `-`, two nights running. A same-night D-1 units read is not reliable, and
   any prediction whose falsifier names ASC units (P28) may be unscoreable on its due date. Say **unpublished**,
   never **zero**, and check whether a due prediction depends on it *before* the due date.
2. **A send is not a delivery, and a delivery is not an open — print all three or say which are unread.**
   The first three `push_sends` rows in the product's history (12:15:02Z 09-16) came back from the sender as
   `accepted 3`, which is Expo's queue acknowledgement. `push_receipts` held **0 rows**. A briefing that wrote
   "3 push delivered" would have been wrong at the first hop. Same family as the 09-02 rule (*an outbound
   result is not a user signal until the artefact has been read*).
3. **An empty table is a measurement failure, not a zero reading — check `count(*)` lifetime before quoting a
   14-day zero.** `tag_corrections` returned 0 rows for 14 days and **0 rows for all time**, while PostHog
   logged `item_tag_corrected` 3 times in 7 days. A zero from a table that has never been written to says
   nothing about users. This is the Supabase-side twin of the 09-08 `ai_calls.outfit_generation` lesson.
4. **A two-day zero at the top of the funnel is an ALERT only with its denominator printed and a
   no-exclusions re-run beside it.** The exclusion CTEs that protect every other number in this file will also
   hide a real person if the filter is wrong. Tonight's falsification pass ran the same window with **no
   exclusions at all** and found only the engineering session's own sim — which is what makes the zero safe to
   print. Do this every time the claim is "nobody came."
5. **When a prior night's briefing is contradicted by an engineering session's own re-read, the correction
   goes in §17 *and* the thesis section that argued from the error is named.** §17.2 tonight. An engineering
   log that says "the 09-15 premise was a misread" is a scoring event against the argument, not only against
   the row.
