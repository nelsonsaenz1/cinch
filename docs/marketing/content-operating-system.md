# Cinch — Content Operating System
**Organic-first, 12 weeks · 10 August 2026**

---

## The correction

The asset manifest allocated $2,000 to Apple Ads. That was over-rotated. Revised
position:

> **Organic finds the format. Paid amplifies a proven one. Never the reverse.**

Apple Ads keeps a small line — $300, three weeks — for one narrow purpose: harvesting
which *search terms* actually convert, which then feeds ASO. It is not an acquisition
channel at this budget. Everything else goes into content.

The evidence for organic-first was in the original research and I under-weighted it:
every founder case study followed the same sequence — *narrow urgent problem → visible
product proof → tracked organic testing → activation proof → paid amplification.* And
the specific opening still stands: TikTok generates `/discover/` pages only for queries
with real volume, and **`whering vs indyx`, `whering vs acloset`, `best digital wardrobe
app` and `capsule wardrobe 2026` all exist right now.** That is confirmed demand,
unclaimed, addressable with a screen recording and no face.

## 1 · Is it labor intensive? Yes. Here is the honest number.

Per finished post, working from a pre-built master-capture library:

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Concept + hook (batched 20 at a time) | ~4 min |
| Screen capture | ~0 — reuses masters |
| Edit in CapCut | 12–15 min |
| Caption, hashtags, sound, upload | 5 min |
| Comment triage | 2 min |
| **Total** | **~23 min** |

At **2 posts/day across 2 accounts = 28/week**, that's **~11 hours a week**, every
week, for twelve weeks minimum, before you know whether it worked.

For scale: the best-documented 0→50K-install TikTok case ran **140 posts/week across 10
accounts for 8 weeks**. You'd be running at 20% of that volume. That's not
disqualifying — but it means the timeline stretches and the variance is high.

**This is a 15 hr/week job.** You have ~14. So it only works if most of it is
delegated, which is what §3 is for.

## 2 · What I can and can't do

Being precise here matters more than being encouraging.

### I can do — roughly 70% of the work

- **Every script, hook, caption, hashtag set, and on-screen text card.** All of it,
  batched weeks ahead.
- **Shot lists and storyboards** — the exact frame-by-frame for each video, so the
  editor is assembling, not deciding.
- **The content calendar and the post queue** — delivered as a spreadsheet plus one
  folder of numbered briefs.
- **Research** — trending sounds and formats, competitor content teardowns, comment
  mining for the exact language people use about this problem.
- **Creator sourcing, vetting, briefs, outreach DMs, and negotiation terms in writing.**
  I'll score candidates on audience fit, median recent views, comment quality and
  disclosure reliability — never follower count.
- **Reviewing creator deliverables** against a rubric before you pay.
- **Performance analysis and the weekly kill/scale decision.**
- **Tracking setup** — App Store campaign tokens, naming conventions, the register.

### I can't do

- **Record or edit video.** No video editing capability, and the master captures have
  to come off your device — you're the only one with the app and a real closet.
- **Post to TikTok, Instagram, or Pinterest.** No connectors for them, and
  browser-automating uploads violates platform terms and puts your accounts at risk.
  Not worth it. A proper scheduler with real API access is the right tool.
- **Handle payments.** You said you'd take this; that's the right split.
- **Appear on camera** — but nothing here requires anyone to.

**The division that follows:** I produce the queue, someone assembles and posts it, you
supply raw captures and approvals.

## 3 · The structure — three roles

### Role 1 · Me — strategy and production. $0.

Scripts, hooks, captions, calendar, briefs, creator pipeline, analysis, weekly
decision. Delivered every Sunday as next week's queue.

### Role 2 · One hire — editor *and* poster. ~$450/month.

The bottleneck role, and the only one you have to pay for. Look for **"social media VA
with video editing"** — this is a common combined skill set, and hiring one person
beats hiring two.

- **~15 hrs/week at $7–8/hr.** Take my briefs → cut in CapCut → schedule → post →
  triage comments → pull Friday's numbers into the tracker.
- **Hire via OnlineJobs.ph** — $69–99/mo subscription, **no per-hire fee**, versus
  Upwork's 20% freelancer + 5% buyer cut. On a $450/mo hire that's ~$110/mo saved.
- Test with a paid trial task: three finished videos from three of my briefs. Judge on
  whether they matched the brief, not on flair.

### Role 3 · You — captures and approvals. ~3–4 hrs/week.

- Record the master captures (§5) — only you can. One 2-hour session covers most of
  the campaign.
- Approve next week's queue on Sunday. 20 minutes.
- Handle payments and account credentials.
- Say no when something misrepresents the product.

## 4 · Platforms — four, in priority order

### 1 · TikTok — the engine
Highest ceiling, confirmed unclaimed search demand, best discovery for a zero-follower
account. **Two accounts:** `@cinchstyle` (main) and a second "closet math" account that
posts only the arithmetic format. Ten accounts beat one in the case data; two is what's
sustainable and still captures most of the multi-account effect.

- 2 posts/day, every day. Consistency beats polish.
- Lowercase, conversational captions with keywords in them — captions are indexed, and
  keyword-bearing captions lift visibility 20–40%.
- Exactly **one soft app mention, placed mid-video.** Never open with the app name.

### 2 · Instagram Reels — same files, different algorithm
Under 1,000 followers Reels get **134 median views vs 56 for carousels** — start with
Reels only, add carousels once there's a following. 694,000 Reels are DM-shared per
minute, so *"send this to the friend who says she has nothing to wear"* is a literal
CTA that exploits the platform's strongest signal.

### 3 · YouTube Shorts — third distribution, near-zero marginal cost
Same vertical file. ⚠️ **Adapt each one — different hook text, different title.**
YouTube's July 2025 policy names *"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal
templates"* and mass-identical reposting as a Partner Program disqualifier.

### 4 · Pinterest — different content type, long fuse
Stills, not video. 640M MAU, 81% mobile, fashion is 18% of platform engagement, 75% of
Pinners in purchase mode. **3–5 fresh pins/day**, 1000×1500. Keyword-titled boards
mirroring the ASO targets. Median save→convert lag is **14 days** — set attribution
windows accordingly or you'll misread it. Honest timeline: 3–6 months to real traffic,
so it lands in Q4 if started now.

⚠️ **Never pin a bare App Store link.** Pinterest's in-app browser won't launch it, and
a raw store link gives Pinterest no on-site engagement signal, which suppresses your
distribution. Pin to a landing page.

### Reddit — listening only
r/femalefashionadvice (5.8M) and r/malefashionadvice (6.4M) ban product posts and a ban
is permanent. Value here is **mining exact problem language** for hooks, plus one
launch-day post in r/SideProject, which is the one permissive venue.

## 5 · The content system

### Master captures — one recording session, reused all quarter

Nine clips from a real device, real closet, real data. Clean status bar, 60fps,
1080×1920. Everything else is cut from these.

The Ten (photographing, 4× speed) · The Reveal · Today · Closet scroll ·
Cost-per-wear · The occasion end-to-end · Packing · Borrow request · Stylist answer.

Add four more in October when voice ships: The Walkthrough · The Reading · The
Procession · The silhouette wall.

### Six formats, rotating

| Format | Why |
|---|---|
| **The comparison** — "everyone recommends Whering, I switched because of this one screen" | Captures the four confirmed head-to-head searches. Highest intent, lowest competition. |
| **The math** — "ten pieces, watch the number" | Yours alone. Nobody can copy it without building your engine. |
| **Cost-per-wear** — "$340 coat, worn twice, here's what that costs per wear" | Whering and Indyx both market CPW; neither owns the search term. |
| **The specific number** — "I photographed 214 items. I wear 31." | Product/Outcome Showcase is the top-performing hook archetype at ~2× the weakest. |
| **The comment magnet** — "is re-wearing an outfit twice in a week bad?" | Drives the comment signal the algorithm reads. |
| **The straight demo** — hook, then 15s of the product doing the thing | The baseline that everything else is measured against. |

### The batching rhythm

- **Pre-launch:** 40–60 clips banked before the first post. Posting daily from an empty
  bank fails in week two — this is the single most common failure mode.
- **Weekly:** I deliver Sunday's queue (14 briefs + captions). Editor cuts Mon–Tue,
  schedules Wed. Friday we read numbers and I rewrite next week around whatever moved.

## 6 · Seeded creators — the accelerant, not the engine

**Do not spend here until week 5.** Seed creators only once your own accounts have
identified a format that works — otherwise you're paying someone to test a hypothesis
you could test for free.

**Buy a post to their audience, not footage for your ads.** Different product, different
vendors:

| Vendor | What it is | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Collabstr** | Marketplace, transparent per-post pricing, no subscription. You buy a post to a creator's audience directly. | ✅ **Best fit.** You're buying reach. |
| **Insense** | Creator marketplace with whitelisting/Spark Ads. Monthly access fee. Turnaround stretched to 3–4 weeks recently. | ⚠️ Good if you later boost |
| **JoinBrands** | Tiered creators, has "post to creator's account" tiers | ⚠️ Viable |
| **Direct DM outreach** | Cheapest, best rates, most work. I write and send these. | ✅ Run alongside Collabstr |
| **Billo / Arcads** | UGC footage for *your* ads. No audience attached. | ❌ Wrong product |

**Terms:** 6 creators at ~$150 each. Score on audience fit, median recent views, comment
quality — **never follower count**. Cap any single creator at $200 without approval.
**Negotiate paid usage rights into the base rate** — rights normally cost 30–50% extra
and whitelisting ~30%/month, and you'll want to boost a winner.

**Non-negotiable:** disclosure must be *in the video*, not buried in the description or
a hashtag block. FTC guidance is explicit. And never condition payment on a positive
review or an App Store rating.

## 9 · Kill criteria — agreed now, not argued in November

Organic is a volume game with a long fuse. Most accounts see nothing for 4–8 weeks, and
the creative-performance data says **only ~5% of posts ever meaningfully outperform** —
volume is how you buy lottery tickets. At 28/week × 12 weeks = 336 posts, a 5% hit rate
is ~17 posts that break out. That's enough to find a format, but only if you actually
reach the volume.

| Checkpoint | Pass | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 4** | Any single post >10K views **and** ≥20 App Store referrals traceable to it | Formats are wrong, not the channel. Rewrite all six. |
| **Week 8** | ≥1 format with repeatable >5K views and measurable installs | If nothing across 200+ posts, the *hook thesis* is wrong. Change the wedge, not the effort. |
| **Week 12** | ≥100 installs/week from organic, or a clearly improving trend | Stop, and reallocate to newsletters + Apple Ads with whatever conversion data exists |

**Measure App Store Connect referral traffic and campaign-token installs — never
views.** The recurring lesson in every case study is that a 120K-view video can produce
single-digit trials.

**Commit to 12 weeks or don't start.** Quitting at week 6 is the same as never having
run it, and costs the same money.

## The one thing that decides this

Not the scripts, not the editing, not the creator budget. **Whether posts still go out
in week nine.**

Every organic case study that worked has the same shape: nothing for weeks, then a step
change. Every one that failed quit somewhere in the flat part. The bank of 40 clips and
the $450/month VA exist for exactly one reason — to make week nine happen without
depending on how you feel that week.
