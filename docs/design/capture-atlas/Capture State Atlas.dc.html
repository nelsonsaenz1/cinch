Cinch · Capture screen · build reference
The Capture state atlas
Every rendering the camera stage can produce, traced from src/app/capture.tsx, use-camera-access.ts and the ingest queue. This is the one dark screen in a light-only app: ink stage, canvas type, light status bar. Three fixed bands — stage, control row, progress sheet — hold their geometry through every state; only the sheet changes height.
01 · Access
Three things the stage can be
Permission is requested on entry, not on the shutter — by the time anyone looks at the stage, iOS has either put its alert over it or the preview is already running. checking and blocked are kept apart on purpose: a user who has never been asked has refused nothing.

02 · The gates before a photo
Capacity, then consent
All three doors — Library, shutter, Describe — dispatch through one guard. Capacity is checked before consent: a full closet means the garment cannot be added at all, so asking permission to send photographs to a model moments before refusing the thing they asked for collects a decision nobody has a use for.

The sheet, as the queue fills
Everything below the control row is read off the real upload queue — never fabricated. The stage stays live throughout, so pieces are shot in sequence and the rail grows underneath them. Crops start at the control row.

05 · The control row
Three doors, and the one that isn’t flip
Third slot
Describe, where flip was drawn
The handoff puts “Flip” opposite Library. A front-camera photograph of a garment held at arm’s length is not a garment photograph, so flip would be a control for a shot nobody wants. Describe is the door for the pieces somebody will never get round to photographing at all — and it passes the same gates, because it ends in the same insert and the same model call.
Sizing
Labels take the width they need
A fixed 64pt box sliced “Library” to “Libra” at AX-1. Both labels carry a minWidth and shrink to fit rather than clip — the label is the only thing that says what the control does. The shutter is a fixed 64pt ring with a 50pt disc at all sizes.
Feedback
Haptics are best-effort
A selection tap fires on all three doors, but haptics no-op during a camera session and in Low Power Mode — the pressed state is the real feedback, and nothing waits on the taptic call.
Leaving
“Done”, not “Close”
An import keeps running in the queue after this screen goes away, so the tap ends the user’s session at the stage rather than closing anything down. A settled batch is cleared on the way out; anything still moving persists and resumes on the next launch.
